# Web Evolution

A term you'll frequently encounter in blockchain is "**Web3**", which refers to the decentralized version of the Internet. But before diving deep into Web3, let's go back to the beginnings of the web and see how it has evolved over the years.&#x20;

### Web 1.0

Also known as the **static web**, it was the first version of the web we had access to. It began in the early 90s and was mainly characterized by only allowing users to read information. Most web pages were simple sites with static content, composed mostly of text and the occasional image.

Think of websites from that era as digital newspapers or magazines, all information came from central sources and users did not have the option to create or publish their own content.

These limitations were due to the web technologies of the time that did not support dynamic interactivity, but they laid the foundation for the creation of more advanced systems in the future.

### Web 2.0

Also known as the **dynamic web**, it marked the next major advancement in the evolution of the internet. It began to take shape in the mid-2000s and was characterized by allowing bidirectional interaction between users and the website. Unlike its predecessor, Web2 not only allowed reading information but also active user interaction.&#x20;

The pages of this era were dynamic and updated in real-time, allowing functions like comments, likes, and the ability to publish your own posts. This opened the door to social platforms where each user could contribute, edit, and share content.

These improvements not only facilitated collaboration and information exchange between users but also stimulated the development of online businesses and social networks. Here, the creation of digital communities became fundamental to the user experience on the web.

Despite all these improvements, Web2 still had a problem: the ownership and control of information and user data remained in the hands of large corporations, which raised serious concerns about user privacy and autonomy on the web.&#x20;

<figure><img src="../.gitbook/assets/image (57).png" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

### Web 3.0

Also known as the **decentralized web**, it represents the third generation of internet evolution. It is characterized mainly by its focus on decentralization and the use of blockchain that allows users to have full control and ownership over their data and assets.

Unlike Web2, where centralized platforms dominated information exchange, Web3 makes it possible for transactions to be executed directly between users without the need for intermediaries.

Imagine the websites of this era as autonomous communities where each participant not only contributes but also has a share of ownership in the platform. This is possible thanks to smart contracts, which ensure transactions and agreements without the need for third parties.

With all this, the goal is to make the web a safer, more transparent, and more equitable place, introducing digital autonomy in an environment where privacy and security are priorities.
